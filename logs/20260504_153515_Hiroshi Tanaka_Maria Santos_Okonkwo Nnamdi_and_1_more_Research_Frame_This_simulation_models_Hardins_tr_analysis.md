# Simulation Analysis Report

**Premise:** Research Frame:
This simulation models Hardin's tragedy of the commons in a real-world
marine resource context, testing Ostrom's design principles for
successful common-pool resource governance. It examines whether
voluntary cooperation can emerge when individual incentives favor
defection and enforcement mechanisms are weak or absent.

Setting:
A coastal community of 85 fishing households depends on a local fishery
that has sustained them for generations. Fish stocks have declined to
40% of historical levels. Marine biologist Dr. Lisa Chen's survey data
shows that current harvest rates of 320 tonnes per season exceed the
maximum sustainable yield of 200 tonnes by 60%. Without intervention,
the fishery will cross an irreversible tipping point within 18 months.

Stakes:
The community council has called an emergency meeting to negotiate
voluntary catch limits. If agreement is reached and enforced, the
fishery can recover to 70% capacity within 3 years. If negotiations
fail, each fisher faces a rational incentive to maximize short-term
catch before the resource collapses entirely — a race to the bottom
that would destroy livelihoods worth $2.4 million annually and
eliminate the community's primary protein source. The neighboring
village of Seaview lost its fishery 10 years ago under identical
conditions and has never recovered economically.

## Executive Summary

This simulation modeled a Hardin-style tragedy of the commons in an 85-household coastal fishery where stocks had fallen to 40% of historical levels and current harvests of 320 tonnes per season exceeded the 200-tonne maximum sustainable yield. The stakes were ecological collapse within 18 months, loss of a $2.4M annual livelihood base, and food insecurity, with Seaview’s prior fishery collapse serving as a warning. The four agents represented different pressures within the governance problem: Dr. Lisa Chen sought adoption of a science-based 200-tonne plan with monitoring and penalties; Hiroshi Tanaka sought a community-wide reduction agreement with at least 80% voluntary compliance; Maria Santos wanted conservation only if it preserved enough income to meet her $3,200 boat loan and $1,800 operating costs; and Okonkwo Nnamdi prioritized feeding his family of six and earning at least $15/day even if agreements threatened those minimums.

The major turning point was the early convergence around a 25% reduction pilot rather than an immediate hard cap. Dr. Chen opened with the scientific warning in Step 1, Maria proposed a gradual voluntary reduction in Step 2, Hiroshi signed onto a 25% reduction pilot with quarterly logbook audits in Step 3, and Okonkwo joined in Step 4 while asking for patrols and rapid-response procedures. Tension emerged in Steps 5–6 when Dr. Chen re-centered the 200-tonne target and Maria resisted locking in a fixed cap without safeguards for small operators. The enforcement architecture then hardened after reports of off-book sales: Hiroshi ordered outbound boats recalled and tied benefits to audited landings in Step 7; Okonkwo helped build the audit trail and penalty ladder in Step 8; Dr. Chen integrated audits, dockside watch, rapid response, and penalties in Step 9; and Maria coordinated evidence collection and regulator involvement in Step 10. Later disruptions tested the system: a storm knocked out power in Step 13, prompting Dr. Chen’s “Manual chain-of-custody” protocol; Maria implemented tamper seals and carbon-copy receipts in Step 14; Hiroshi managed storm procedures and responded to possible Seaview incursions in Step 15; Okonkwo helped distinguish GPS firmware bugs from cheating in Step 16; and by Step 18 the audit trail remained intact and progress toward the 200-tonne cap was reported as “on track.”

Goal attainment was mixed but generally positive. Dr. Chen substantially achieved her goal in procedural terms: the log shows the 200-tonne cap repeatedly reaffirmed, quarterly audits, dockside monitoring, rapid-response procedures, a penalty ladder, provisional suspensions, and manual chain-of-custody rules. However, the log does not prove full community adoption across all 85 households. Hiroshi partially achieved his goal: he secured visible cooperation from all named fishing agents, created recall and audit mechanisms, and advanced toward the 200-tonne target, but the stated threshold of “at least 80% voluntary compliance from fishing households” is never measured or confirmed. Maria partially achieved, and perhaps nearly achieved, her goal: she supported conservation while insisting on safeguards, and the Step 18 outcome says the plan stayed on track “without jeopardizing the earnings she needed,” but the text is truncated and gives no actual income, loan-payment, or operating-cost figures. Okonkwo partially achieved his goal: he joined the pilot and helped enforcement while still focusing on practical livelihood constraints, but the log never confirms that he fed his family of six or earned at least $15/day.

Several emergent dynamics appeared beyond a simple cooperate/defect choice. The agents formed a practical enforcement coalition: Hiroshi handled recall and incentives, Maria coordinated documentation and regulator contact, Okonkwo managed dockside details and safety queues, and Dr. Chen supplied scientific framing and protocol design. Rather than relying solely on weak voluntary promises, they created layered governance tools consistent with Ostrom-style principles: monitored boundaries, audited landings, graduated penalties, rapid response, benefits for compliance, and local participation. Maria’s cognitive-bias component was visible in her repeated attention to debt-service risk and reluctance to accept a rigid cap without safeguards; Okonkwo’s theory-of-planned-behavior framing was visible in his conditional cooperation once patrols, incentives, and monitoring made compliance seem feasible; Hiroshi’s values appeared in his emphasis on community-wide compliance and avoiding unauthorized runs; and Dr. Chen’s science-oriented personality was reflected in her insistence on data, checkpoints, and enforceable penalties. Observation-action coherence was mostly strong: agents responded logically to perceived off-book sales, power failures, GPS discrepancies, and enforcement gaps. However, the observation logs contain perspective inconsistencies: for example, Dr. Chen’s Step 5 observation is written from Hiroshi’s seat, Dr. Chen’s Step 13 observation describes Maria’s viewpoint, and Dr. Chen’s Step 17 observation follows Hiroshi at the radio console. These mismatches make some perception-action links ambiguous, even though the narrated actions themselves generally fit the shared situation.

The final outcome says progress toward the 200-tonne cap “stayed on track,” but the log does not provide actual catch totals, compliance rates, household income impacts, fish stock changes, or whether the 80% voluntary compliance target was met.

For a research simulation, it would be valuable to track:
